\title{ALMA: A Health Monitor That Says When It Cannot See\\
\large Preregistered Falsification of Body--Habitat Monitoring for Crewed Missions}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Team ALMA (TONO-LAB)}
\IEEEauthorblockA{NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 --- ``Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions''\\
Repository: \url{https://github.com/userly03/ALMA}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Open data on astronaut health are almost nonexistent, so a monitor cannot be validated on the people it is meant to protect. We therefore built ALMA around a different promise: every alarm prints the exact rule, seed and preregistration hash that produced it, and every ``all clear'' declares the smallest effect it could have detected. We tested each component with 30 hash-sealed experiments (25 executed so far, each once), on real wearable, blood-count, neutron-monitor and ECG data and on a labelled simulated habitat. Three results lead. (i)~In a simulated habitat, a conservation-of-mass check flags a 0.30\,kg air leak (residual $+0.260$\,kg) that a manifest-based detector scores at 0.53\sd{} and misses. (ii)~On the only open astronaut blood data (four Inspiration4 crew), a personal band built from three baseline draws cannot see a median 24\% fall in lymphocytes: the band's detection floor is about $\pm$70\% of the personal mean. (iii)~The same CUSUM used for bodies, applied unchanged to a neutron monitor, catches 41\% of severe geomagnetic storms against 6\% by chance, but 13\,h after the storm index crosses storm level: it confirms, it does not forecast. Most hypotheses we generated were refuted, and we publish them all.
\end{abstract}

astronaut health monitoring, personal baseline, CUSUM, conservation of mass, preregistration, negative results, detection floor

\section{Introduction}
Crewed missions beyond low Earth orbit will have limited ground support: a one-way communication delay of up to about 22 minutes for Mars~\cite{mcbrayer2022}, no real-time specialist, and a crew too small to average over. Monitoring must therefore run onboard and personalise: each person is compared with their own signature~\cite{zheng2023,scott2023}. The obstacle is evidence. Per-person astronaut data are not openly available; what NASA's Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) does release for human spaceflight is a handful of studies, among them blood counts of four civilian astronauts. Any health-monitoring system built today is therefore validated on proxies, and the risk is that proxies flatter it.

We take the position that, under these constraints, a monitor's most valuable property is \emph{knowing what it cannot see}. ALMA (Spanish for ``soul''; working name TONO-LAB) has two hearts. The \emph{Guardian} watches the crew's bodies, the habitat's books and the space environment. \emph{The Foundry} is a laboratory that coins hypotheses, tests each once under a hash sealed beforehand, and publishes the outcome; a hypothesis that survives compiles into a new detector for the Guardian. We report what the system is, how every claim was made, and, first and in detail, what held and what failed.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1)~A method of work: preregistration with a SHA-256 hash committed before execution, one run per act, public seeds, a control that can fail, and negative results published. (2)~A receipt contract: alarms and ``all clears'' that carry their own rule, seed, hash, power and minimum detectable effect. (3)~Thirty sealed experiments (25 executed so far) and 16 hypothesis cards, 10 of them refuted. (4)~An open, offline-capable interface that separates real from simulated data on every screen.
We claim no algorithmic novelty (Section~\ref{sec:related}); the contribution is the application, the measurement of what each detector cannot see, and the discipline.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\emph{Change detection.} The CUSUM chart~\cite{page1954} is the detector ALMA uses for bodies, habitat books and cosmic rays. Individual CUSUMs on physiological series date to the 1990s~\cite{cornelissen1997}. Anytime-valid inference and e-processes~\cite{ramdas2023,shin2022} offer false-alarm guarantees under continuous monitoring; we measured how those guarantees fare on real wearable data (Section~\ref{sec:neg}).
\emph{Conservation and reconciliation.} Data reconciliation against balances to detect gross errors is a classical process-engineering method~\cite{tamhane1985}; mass-balance diagnosis already exists for spacecraft cabin air~\cite{kayatin2021}. We apply the idea daily to a crewed habitat, with the manifest's own uncertainty as the detection floor.
\emph{Personal baselines and wearables.} Baseline-and-deviation monitoring with wearables is an active field, including for astronauts~\cite{gupta2025,zheng2023}; reliability manifests of the ISS have been off by 1.9--22$\times$~\cite{jones2019}, which motivates not trusting a manifest alone. Alarm fatigue is a known hazard~\cite{cvach2012}; it motivates ``green only with a receipt''.
\emph{Search scope.} We searched PubMed and the NASA Technical Reports Server directly and the web; IEEE Xplore and Google Scholar were not queried, and the search is not systematic. ``We found nothing'' is not evidence of absence.

\section{System}
Data flow is one-directional: ingestion $\to$ ledger $\to$ engine $\to$ factory $\to$ surfaces, and nothing reads a sensor directly. Fourteen data contracts (DC-01\ldots DC-14) define every record. Three commandments are enforced in code and tests:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Everything reads from the ledger} (contract DC-04, nine budgets: air, water, energy, dose, rhythm, load, immunity, conduct, time) or from event contracts, never from raw sensors.
\item \textbf{Nothing is tested without a preregistration hash} made before running; a test checks that every act is committed before its result.
\item \textbf{Colours only with a contestable rule.} Every alarm carries its rule text, seed and hash (DC-05); a green is a receipt (DC-14) that declares its power and minimum detectable effect, or it is ``not enough days''. A REAL/SIMULATED banner is mandatory on every record and screen, and a derived record never upgrades to REAL silently.
\end{enumerate}
The interface is a web application with nine rooms (medical module, machine room, the leak, the vault, the foundry, the storm, the budget, the capsule, the frontier), a deterministic narrator whose sentences are templates plus data values (it is not a language model), and an offline mode. The crew is fictional (five generated characters with stand-in body states), and the habitat is a labelled simulated universe anchored to public NASA planning values.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Personal baseline and detectors.} Each indicator is compared with the person's own baseline; the two-sigma residual and the bilateral CUSUM $S^{\pm}_t=\max(0,S^{\pm}_{t-1}\pm z_t-k)$ with $k=0.5$ raise a flag when $S^{\pm}\geq h$. The threshold $h$ is calibrated by Monte Carlo with a public seed so that the false-alarm probability over 14 days is 1\%, including the uncertainty of estimating the baseline from 14 days.\\
\textbf{Conservation closure.} For each loop (water, air, energy), inputs minus outputs minus storage must equal the declared loss within the manifest's propagated uncertainty (``closes'' / ``does not close'').\\
\textbf{Detection floor.} Every ``all clear'' declares the minimum detectable effect (MDE) in $\sigma$ and in physical units; with too little data it states ``not enough days''.\\
\textbf{Preregistration.} Each act states the question, the success rule and the kill rule, and is hashed (SHA-256 of its exact bytes) and committed \emph{before} its runner can execute; runners refuse an altered act, different data, or a second run. Public seeds are 20261113, 20261114, 20261115, 20261111 and 20261112; the derived family $20261113+i$ is used only for regression repeats. Each act includes a control that can fail, and the authors' own predictions are recorded and reported when wrong.\\
\textbf{Statistics.} Within-person contrasts use label shuffling inside each person; correlations use circular shifts; Bonferroni corrections are fixed in the act; replication cohorts are independent.

\section{Results (strongest first)}
\subsection{A conservation law sees what a manifest cannot (simulated)}
In the labelled simulated habitat (\act{003R}, \act{005-D1}), on a day with an injected 0.30\,kg air leak, a detector that compares the book with the manifest scores 0.53\sd{} and raises no alarm. The conservation check on the same day, with the manifest's propagated uncertainty $\sigma=0.0548$\,kg, shows a residual of $+0.260$\,kg and ``does not close''. On a control day both are quiet; the prediction was recorded beforehand and confirmed. This is a simulation: no open dataset of a crewed cabin exists, and the model of the flows is ours.

\subsection{The detection floor in real astronaut blood work}
We reconciled the open complete-blood-count data of four civilian astronauts of the Inspiration4 mission (OSD-569; seven draws each) against themselves (absolute count = percentage $\times$ leukocytes): 1 of 28 rows does not close, a probable data-entry error in public data (\act{024}). On landing day the median lymphocyte count fell 24\%. Yet with three preflight draws each, no astronaut left their personal band: the hypothesis that at least two of four would is refuted (0 of 4). Pooling the group's within-person variance narrows the bands by about 30\% (median ratio 1.42), but the detection floor for lymphocytes stays near $\pm$70\% of the personal mean, almost three times the median fall (\act{025}, exploratory, same data). The consequence is the central design rule of ALMA: before alarming, declare what the baseline can see. With $n=4$ and no in-flight draws, this is a demonstration of the floor, not a clinical result.

\subsection{Alarms with receipts, experiments with hashes}
Of 30 sealed acts, 25 have been executed, each once; their order of commit versus result is checked by a test. The receipt contract is implemented and tested, but real green receipts cover only heart rate and sleep from a consumer wearable cohort. \subsection{A laboratory that generates and closes its own hypotheses}
A library of mechanisms with identified sources is crossed with the data that actually exist, producing candidates with a price and a refuting signal; the generator also drafts the preregistration from the same specification the runner uses, so what is sealed and what runs cannot diverge. The first complete loop (\act{026}; LifeSnaps, everyday wearers, not astronauts) gave: sleeping below one's own median lowers overnight heart-rate variability (RMSSD) by 0.21 sd (36 people, $p=0.0001$, placebo with labels shifted by 40 days null) --- sustained; the matching resting-pulse effect (+0.25 bpm, $p=0.003$) is real but below the sealed minimum of 0.5 bpm --- refuted by the written rule; same-row training load shows no effect. A second batch (\act{027}) produced four refutations: warmer nights and a higher stress score went with \emph{better} sleep efficiency (opposite to what we sealed; we had not verified the polarity of the score, which includes sleep as an input), geomagnetic activity showed no link with breathing, and next-night HRV after hard days fell by the expected 0.08 sd but at $p=0.029$, above the corrected threshold. An independent cohort (\act{028}, 169 people) shrank the pulse effect to +0.11 bpm ($p=0.0325$): refuted under both sealed rules.

\subsection{Cosmic rays as a second witness}\label{sec:cosmic}
Applying the \emph{same} CUSUM, unchanged, to the hourly pressure-corrected neutron-monitor series of Oulu (NMDB~\cite{nmdb}, 2008--2025) against severe geomagnetic storms (Kp $\geq 7$; GFZ index~\cite{matzka2021}), with $h$ calibrated only on 2008--2016, we tested 2017--2025 (\act{029}). It detects 9 of 22 storms (41\%; chance 6\%, $p=0.001$; exact 95\% interval 21--64\%) with 4.9 false alarms per year, so the false-alarm and placebo hypotheses hold; the 50\% sensitivity target and the lead-time target fail, with a median of $-13$\,h: the neutron monitor confirms a storm already under way. It cannot replace an early-warning ladder.

\subsection{The space around the crew, live}
The interface reads, with a raw-response hash and an archive, the ISS position (derived from orbital elements), GOES protons and electrons, solar X-rays, the planetary K index, official NOAA notices, and the neutron monitor, and computes NOAA scale levels with printed rules. None is a dose measured on board, and the interface says so. A real ECG record (MIT-BIH record 100~\cite{moody2001}) is annotated by our R-peak detector: 2\,272 of 2\,273 beats found (sensitivity 99.96\%, no invented beats) on one clean record.

\section{Negative Results and Our Own Errors}\label{sec:neg}
These results are part of the method. (1)~The variance channel had 9.0\% false alarms on real data (\act{020}); a robust variant was inconclusive (5 validation windows, \act{020-v2}). (2)~A ``10 of 10 detections, 21-day lead'' result that we could not reproduce: our replication detected 8 of 10 (\act{015-v2}). (3)~A control we wrote could not fail (\act{010}). (4)~The theoretical false-alarm guarantee of an e-process was five times optimistic on real wearables: 5.06\% observed against 1\% requested; calibration restores 0\% at the cost of power at $2\sigma$, from 99\% to 71\% (\act{021}); out-of-sample validation had only 5 windows. (5)~Temperature, breathing and RMSSD do couple with resting pulse within person out of sample ($\rho=+0.24$, $+0.27$, $-0.53$), but a classifier to tell a broken sensor from a changed body failed (66\% accuracy; 44\% of untouched real blocks flagged) because RMSSD and pulse share one optical sensor (\act{022}). (6)~Attribution of excess cabin CO$_2$ to one crew member works only with staggered schedules (71\% at best against 20\% chance, simulated) and our 80\% target was missed (\act{023}). (7)~Solar storms against sleep and HRV: null (\act{014}). (8)~Our predictions for \act{011}, \act{022}, \act{026}, \act{027} and \act{029} were wrong in at least one hypothesis, and a sealed stress hypothesis failed because we did not check a score's polarity.

\section{Limitations}
We make \emph{no} claim that ALMA detects disease or has been validated on astronauts. Real data come from everyday people with consumer devices; astronaut data are four civilians, none sampled in flight. Habitat data are simulated, and where we could measure real data the simulation's assumptions were often optimistic (water-demand correlation across households $\approx0.13$; pulse autocorrelation $\approx0.18$). Several results are inconclusive by sample size. Where an act extended earlier work on the same data, it says so. The anatomical layer of the interface is illustrative: a segmented reference body (BodyParts3D~\cite{bp3d}) deformed to fictional characters. ALMA is not a language model and not a medical device.

\section{Reproducibility and Ethics}
Code, seeds and data sources are public in the repository. \texttt{uv run pytest} re-derives the contracts (475 tests), and each runner script reproduces its act exactly; the interface runs offline. Every figure in this report traces to a row of \texttt{docs/actas/RESULTADOS.jsonl}, its preregistered act and its note; every data source, licence and simulated component is listed in \texttt{docs/politica-de-datos.md}. Personal data are civilian and anonymised; the crew is fictional; per-person content never reaches the decision surfaces, only aggregates and states; a differential-privacy ledger shows that with five people an exact average plus one subtraction names someone. \section{Future Work}
